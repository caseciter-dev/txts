UNION OF INDIA & ORS. RESPONDENT(S)
O R D E R
1. Delay condoned.
2. The appellant has filed an Original Application (OA) before
the National Green Tribunal, Central Zonal Bench, Bhopal (NGT),
alleging blatant disregard of the conditions stipulated in the
Environmental Clearance (EC) and Consent to Operate (CTO) by
respondent  No.7,  to  whom  the  Mining  Lease  No.323/1997  for
extraction of masonry stone in Village Tanwara, District Nagaur had
been granted.
3. Respondent No.7 had originally obtained CTO from Rajasthan
State Pollution Control Board on 20.11.2023, followed by EC granted
by the SEIAA on 14.06.2024.  
4. The OA was subsequently filed by the appellant, alleging that
respondent No.7 was carrying out mining activities, including deep-
hole blasting, within a close distance of 300 meters from the
residential areas, which was endangering the nearby public and
private property, besides causing environmental damage. Violations
of the conditions contained in the CTO and the EC were also
alleged.
1

--- Page 31 ---
5. The  NGT  constituted  a  Joint  Committee,  comprising
representatives  of  the  Central  Pollution  Control  Board  and
Rajasthan State Pollution Control Board, to inspect the mining site
and examine the allegations. The Joint Committee conducted a site
inspection on 23.07.2025 and undertook an elaborate and extensive
survey of the entire area. In its comprehensive report, the Joint
Committee found that respondent No.7 possessed a valid EC and CTO,
and there was no instance of non-compliance with the conditions
contained in those statutory permissions. No illegal mining was
found to have taken place. The Joint Committee also found that
instead of deep-hole blasting, only shallow-hole blasting was
practised.
6. Nevertheless,  the  Joint  Committee,  keeping  in  view  the
environmental issues and the vicinity of the village and its
residents, made certain recommendations contained in Paragraph 14.0
of its report, which the NGT has also reproduced in Paragraph 6 of
the impugned judgment. The recommendations are to the following
effect:
“14.0 Recommendations
i. Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS) may be
directed to conduct a detailed vibration/seismic study
to assess cumulative impact during operation of all 07
mines at a time.
ii. All leaseholders to install adequate hooter/siren
systems with standardized, high-decibel, multi-point
alarm systems, compliant with DGMS safety norms.
All the leaseholders shall optimize the frequency and
interval of blasting events to allow adequate dust
settling and minimize cumulative vibration and noise
impacts.
2

--- Page 32 ---
iv. All the leaseholders shall provide wind-braking
wall  of  MS  sheets  around  the  mine  lease  boundary
excluding entry & exit point to prevent unauthorized
entry and mitigate probable dust emission and ensure
safety.
Department  of  Mines  and  Geology  and  RSPCB  may  be
directed to take action on M/s Bajrang Lal S/o Kesar
ram (ML no. 48/2005) against exceeding its production
in FY 2023-24.
v. RSPCB need to conduct periodic Ambient air & noise
monitoring in and around the mining cluster and other
prominent locations.”
7. The NGT, while disposing of the OA, however, has restricted
its  directions  only  to  the  extent  of  directing  the  Project
Proponent, with the object of dust suppression, to increase the
plantation on the available land around the mining area and to
ensure the survival of the saplings for up to three years. The
State Pollution Control Board has been directed to periodically
monitor the plantation and its survival and take necessary action
in the event of non-compliance.
8. The aggrieved appellant has filed this statutory appeal.
9. We are conscious of the fact that neither any notice has been
issued  to  respondent  No.7-Project  Proponent,  nor  have  the
authorities been called upon to respond to the appeal at this
stage.However, it seems to us that it would be in the interest of
the village community and the Project Proponent, particularly for
the betterment of the environmental conditions in and around the
area, that all the recommendations made by the Joint Committee are
given effect by the authorities.
3

--- Page 33 ---
10. Consequently, we dispose of this appeal with a direction to
(i) the State of Rajasthan; (ii) the Rajasthan State Pollution
Control Board; (iii) the District Collector, Didwana-Kuchaman,
Rajasthan; and (iv) the Project Proponent, respondent No.7, to
ensure  that  the  remaining  recommendations  made  by  the  Joint
Committee in Paragraph 14.0 of their final report are also given
effect to. For this, the State Pollution Control Board will draw up
a necessary plan for implementation of the recommendations in a
time-bound manner and put up such plan before the NGT. The NGT may
ensure necessary compliance with the instant order.
11. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand closed.
