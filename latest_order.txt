2. We have heard the Learned Senior Counsels appearing for the parties. As it
is at the stage of considering applications for contempt filed for direction,
initiation of contempt for alleged wilful disobedience of the order dated
20.11.2026, we do not propose to dwell upon the lengthy and elaborate
arguments and submissions made on behalf of the parties. 
3. Suffice to state that Learned Senior Counsels appearing for the Petitioners
Shri. Guru Krishna Kumar, Shri. J. Sai Deepak, Shri. P. Valliappan and Shri
Sri Ram have contended as follows:
3.1 That there is flagrant disobedience of the order of this Court dated:
20.11.2026.
3.2 That the State Authorities in connivance with their high political bosses
have planned to avoid the compliance of the order passed by this Court.
3.3 The Executive Authorities have failed to comply with the directions and
therefore the administration of the Devasthanam which is headed by the
Trustees have to be superseded.
3.4 The Deepam that had to be lit at Deepathoon on 24.11.2026, could not
be lit on account of design of the alleged contemnors, therefore, this
Court may direct the lighting of the lamp at Deepathoon in the Karthiga
Month of Tamil Calander. 
4. Shri.  Tushar  Mehta,  the  Learned  Solicitor  General  appearing  for  the
Archaeological Survey of India, in support of the abovesaid submissions
reiterating the same has also contended that the administration of the Temple
has to be carried out by a neutral person to be appointed by this Court and in
place  of  the  local  police,  any  other  central  agency,  may  be  directed  to
administer  the  security  particularly  in  the  teeth  of  ASI  official  being
prevented from entering the place of Deepathoon.

6. Having heard the Learned Senior Counsels appearing for the parties, the
first thing which comes to our mind is to express the word Alas! As it
would be a relevant and an appropriate expression to be used in the present
scenario. We say so for the myriad reasons assigned hereinafter and we say
so with pain and anguish. Time has come to deal with such situations with
iron hand as otherwise it may lead to Constitutional chaos. It is apposite to
note the observations of this Court in T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad v.
Ashok Khot1, which reads as under:
“3. In our democratic polity under the Constitution based on the
concept  of  Rule  of  law’ which  we  have  adopted  and  given  to
ourselves and which serves as an aorta in the anatomy of our
democratic system. THE LAW IS SUPREME.
4. Everyone whether individually or collectively is unquestionably
under the supremacy of law. Whoever he may be, however high he
is, he is under the law. No matter how powerful he is and how rich
he may be.

5. Disobedience of this Court’s order strikes at the very root of the
rule of law on which the judicial  system rests. The rule of law is
the foundation of a democratic society. Judiciary is the guardian
of the rule of law. Hence, it is not only the third pillar but also the
central  pillar  of  the  democratic  State.  If  the  judiciary  is  to
perform its duties and functions effectively and remain true to the
spirit with which they are sacredly entrusted to it, the dignity and
authority of the Courts have to be respected and protected at all
costs.  Otherwise,  the  very  cornerstone  of  our  constitutional
scheme will give way and with it will disappear the rule of law and
the civilized life in the society. That is why it is imperative and
invariable that Court’s orders are to be followed and complied
with.”
7. The concept of rule of law is supreme in a democratic society. The preamble
of the Constitution of India reflects the contours whereunder the people of
India i.e. Bharat have solemnly resolved to secure to all its citizens Justice,
Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.
8. Article  144  of  the  Constitution  mandates  that  all  authorities,  civil  and
judicial, in the territory of India shall act in aid of the Supreme Court. Any
attempt, direct or indirect, to undermine this constitutional mandate, should
be firmly curbed, without any misplaced sympathy. Any attempt to pollute
the stream of justice, by any person howsoever high he maybe, would not be
tolerated, is the message which we intend to convey by this Order.  An order
of this Court is not an invitation to negotiate. It binds the State, its officers
and  every  statutory  or  religious  institution  functioning  under  the  State’s
laws, until this Court itself modifies it. Therefore, when an order of this
Court is flouted, we cannot sit in silos. This Court being a Court of Record
under Article  129 of the Constitution holds sufficient power to bring to
Justice when infraction of the majesty of law occurs. For such cases, the

Contempt  of  Courts  Act,  1971  is  engrafted  by  the  legislature  to  instill
confidence in the people of this Country by imbibing a promise that the
administration of Justice will not bow down to any authority whatsoever.
The Act is a powerful arsenal in the hands of the Constitutional Courts and it
would not shy away to utilize it where the administration of justice is the
necessity of the hour.
9. The present litigation, has a checkered history and undisputedly there is a
decree that has been passed in O.S.No.04 of 1920 and confirmed by the Privy
Council vide judgment dated 12.05.1931. Yet several litigations have cropped
up and the jurisdictional High Court has passed the impugned order dated
06.01.2026, which is subject matter of this litigation. This Court having
heard  the  parties  at  length  and  after  bestowing  careful  and  anxious
consideration to the rival contentions raised at the Bar passed an order on
20.11.2026 whereunder this Court not only stayed the observations made
under the Impugned Order (06.01.2026) at para 1-141 and simultaneously
directed the lighting of the lamp atop the hill at the place referred to by both
the parties as “Deepathoon”,  the relevant portion of the order is extracted
herein below:
“13. Notwithstanding the above the order of the stay of findings passed
hereinabove, we deem it appropriate to mould the interim relief in the
peculiar facts and circumstances of the present case. Hence, we consider it
appropriate to permit the lighting of the ceremonial lamp on 24.11.2026 at
the stone pillar identified as Deepathoon in Annexure-B to the judgment
dated  06.01.2026,  through  a  restricted  team  authorised  by  the
Devasthanam,  subject  to  the  safeguards  indicated  hereinafter.  This
permission is granted as a measure of temporary arrangement and not in
recognition  or  enforcement  of  any  asserted  customary,  religious  or
proprietary right.
14. The following arrangements shall govern the aforesaid ceremony:

(i) The lighting of the lamp shall be undertaken exclusively by
a  team  of  persons  authorised  by  the  Devasthanam.  The
composition and number of such persons shall be settled by
the  District  Collector,  Madurai,  in  consultation  with  the

(iii) No member of the general public shall accompany the
authorised  team  to  the  Deepathoon.  No  procession,  public
congregation,  demonstration,  slogan-raising  or  activity
extraneous  to the ceremonial lighting  of the lamp shall  be
permitted at the site. Access to the ‘Deepathoon’ shall remain
confined to the authorised persons and such officials as may
be necessary for supervision and security.
(iv) The  District  Administration,  in  consultation  with  the
concerned authorities, shall settle the timing, route of access,
duration and other incidental arrangements, keeping in view
the  requirements  of  public  safety,  fire  prevention,
archaeological  protection  and  maintenance  of  peace.  The
authorised  team  shall  leave  the  site  immediately  upon
completion  of  the  ceremony,  without  leaving  behind  any
temporary article or installation.
(v) The  Archaeological  Survey  of  India  shall  prescribe
appropriate conditions for the protection and preservation of
the monument and its surroundings. The ceremony shall be
undertaken strictly in accordance with the applicable statutory
requirements.  No  excavation,  construction,  structural
alteration, permanent installation or damage to any monument,
religious structure or property shall be permitted.

(vi) The  existing  religious  observances  at  the  customary
locations  shall  continue  undisturbed.  Equally,  the  lawful
religious activities, access and enjoyment of the Dargah and
other institutions shall not be interfered with in any manner.
The present arrangement shall not confer upon any person an

15. We make it abundantly clear that the present arrangement does not
proceed upon any finding that the stone pillar is, or has historically been, a
place of customary religious observance. Nor does it determine whether
the  lighting  of  the  lamp  at  the  said  location  constitutes  an  essential
religious practice or whether any individual, religious denomination or
institution  possesses an enforceable right in that  regard.  All  questions
concerning  title,  possession,  customary  usage,  religious  entitlement,
