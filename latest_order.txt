1. Leave granted.
2. We have heard  Mr. Rahul Kaushik,  learned Senior
counsel for the appellant and  Ms. Archana Pathak Dave,
learned Addl. Solicitor General for the respondent-Union
of India.
3. Appellant is aggrieved by order dated 28.07.2026
passed by the High Court of Madhya Pradesh at Indore
(briefly the ‘High Court’ hereinafter), rejecting the
prayer for anticipatory bail of the appellant.
4. Appellant had approached the High Court upon receipt
of  notice  dated  07.05.2026  under  Section  67  of  the
Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 (for
short, NDPS Act) to join the investigation in connection
with  Crime  No.01/2026/C.B.N./Ujjain  registered  under
Sections 8/22 and 29 of the NDPS Act.
1

--- Page 30 ---
5. Insofar the appellant is concerned, the accusation
against  him  is  that  he  was  supplying  2-Bromo-4-
Methlypropiophenone  to  the  co-accused  without  any
supporting trading bills/vouchers. On the basis of the
disclosure statement made by the co-accused Prakash Bhai,
the aforesaid notice was issued to the appellant, for
which the appellant had gone to the High Court.
6. This Court, by order dated 10.09.2026, issued notice
and directed the appellant to respond to the notice of the
Central  Narcotics  Bureau  and  to  appear  before  the
investigating authorities on 17.09.2026 at 11 AM and join
the investigation. Subject to such co-operation, interim
protection was granted to the appellant.
7. Following  the  notice  issued  by  this  Court,
respondent-Union of India has filed counter affidavit
which we have perused.
8. Mr. Rahul Kaushik, learned Senior counsel for the
appellant has basically urged two grounds. The first
ground is based on the decision of this Court in Tofan
Singh versus State of T.N. [(2021) 4 SCC 1] by contending
that the statement recorded under Section 67 of the NDPS
Act (in this case made by the co-accused) cannot be used
as a confessional statement in the trial and on that
basis, appellant cannot be implicated in this case. He has
also placed reliance on certain decisions of this Court
including in the case of  Vijay Singh versus State of
Haryana [2023 SCC Online SC 1235], where the petitioner,
2

--- Page 31 ---
who was implicated on the basis of the confessional
statement made by the co-accused, was granted anticipatory
bail by this Court.
8.1 His other submission is that the contraband, i.e. 2-
Bromo-4-Methlypropiophenone,  came  to  be  included  in
Schedule-A  to  the  Narcotic  Drugs  and  Psychotropic
Substances (Regulation of Controlled Substances) Order,
2013 at Serial No.8 as well as in Schedule-B and Schedule-
C thereof after Serial No.45 at Serial No.46, vide the
notification dated 11.03.2026 issued by the Department of
Revenue, Ministry of Finance, Government of India. On the
above basis, he submits that when the interception and
seizure  were  made  in  January,  2026,  the  above  two
commodities were not prohibited substances under the NDPS
Act. Therefore, appellant cannot be prosecuted, not to
speak of arrest, for an act which was not a prohibited act
when it was committed.
9. Opposing  the  submissions  made  on  behalf  of  the
appellant,  Ms.  Archana  Pathak  Dave,  learned  Addl.
Solicitor General for the respondent-Union of India has
placed reliance on a decision of this Court in 2022 SCC
OnLine SC 2087 [State of Haryana versus Samarth Kumar],
where  this  Court  had  clarified  that  the  respondent
(accused) may take advantage of the decision in  Tofan
Singh’s  case, perhaps at the time of the arguing the
regular bail application or at the time of final hearing,
after conclusion of trial; grant of anticipatory bail in a
3

--- Page 32 ---
NDPS matter was not warranted. Insofar the notification
dated 11.03.2026 is concerned, she has placed reliance on
the averments made in the counter affidavit as well as on
paragraph Nos.8, 11 and 16 of the impugned order of the
High Court to contend that the contraband is used in the
manufacture of Mephedrone drug, which is a prohibited
substance under the NDPS Act. Therefore, though the seized
substance  came  to  be  prohibited  pursuant  to  the
notification dated 11.03.2026, still the person carrying
on trade or any transaction of such substances, that too
without any invoices and bills, would face prosecution
under Section 29 of the NDPS Act. She submits that since
the country is facing the menace of drug addiction on a
large scale, the Court should be slow in granting the
benefit  of  anticipatory  bail  to  an  accused  facing
accusations under the NDPS Act.
10. Submissions made by learned counsel for the parties
have received the due consideration of the Court.
11. The submissions of Ms. Archana Pathak Dave, learned
Addl. Solicitor General for the respondent-Union of India
are well meaning and well intended, which is acknowledged.
But, at the same time, the Court has also to look into the
related  provisions  of  the  statute  as  well  as  the
constitutional limitations. It goes without saying that
more severe the accusations are, the bar becomes that much
higher for the investigation and prosecution.
12. Article 20(1) of the Constitution of India makes it
4

--- Page 33 ---
abundantly clear that there cannot be any retrospectivity
of criminal law. A person cannot be accused and prosecuted
for committing an act which was not an offence on the date
when the act had taken place. Any departure therefrom
would amount to diluting the constitutional limitations
under  Article  20(1)  of  the  Constitution  which  is  a
fundamental right of an accused.

14. A perusal of the aforesaid notification makes it
clear that the same came into force on the date of its
publication in the official gazette, which is 11.03.2026.
On and from the said date, the substance 2-Bromo-4-
Methlypropiophenone, has been included in the prohibited

(Regulation of Controlled Substances) Order, 2013. Even in

6

India, it is clearly stated that the prosecution case

15. Since  we  are  at  the  stage  of  consideration  of
anticipatory bail, we do not want to give any definite
opinion  in  this  regard  in  the  absence  of  detailed
pleadings and a full-fledged hearing but it is clear that
when the seizure was made, the seized substance was not
included in the prohibited list.
16. At this stage, we had put a query to Mr. Rahul
Kaushik, learned Senior counsel for the appellant, who
submits that pursuant to the interim protection granted by
this  Court,  appellant  has  appeared  before  the
investigating authority. However,  Ms. Archana Pathak
Dave, learned Addl. Solicitor General for the respondent-
Union of India submits that the appellant has not handed
over any of the documents in his possession including the
mobile phone to the investigating agency; therefore, it
cannot be said that appellant has co-operated with the
investigation.
17. While reiterating the right of an accused to remain
silent  and  the  right  against  self  incrimination,  as
provided under Article 20(3) of the Constitution of India,
it is for the investigating agency, by application of its
investigative skills, to extract information from the
accused for the purpose of investigation. We need not
delve more on this aspect.

18. Thus, we feel that a case for anticipatory bail is
made out.

Agarwal is concerned and make our order dated 10.09.2026
absolute, subject to the continued co-operation of the
