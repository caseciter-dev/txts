                             O R D E R
1. The High Powered Enquiry Committee (“HPEC”) has submitted an  

--- Page 11 ---
10
Action Taken Report dated 31.10.2026, which has been opened and  
perused. According to the Report, the HPEC decided at its first  
meeting, held on 01.09.2026, to create an online portal for  
receiving complaints. The portal has been developed by the National  
Informatics Centre with the support of the Ministry of Home  
Affairs, and was launched at hpec.nic.in on 13.10.2026.
 
2. It has been stated that complaints filed confidentially on the  
portal are accessible only to the Chairperson and the Members of  
the HPEC. It has further been stated that complaints can also be  
filed anonymously, without disclosing the complainant's name, phone  
number, address, or Aadhaar details. The portal also provides  
separate windows and templates for affected protestors and for  
affected police personnel.
3. Pursuantly,  a  bilingual  public  notice  was  published  on  
14.10.2026 in 149 newspaper editions across India. It provided for  
submissions through the portal, by post, by e-mail, or physically  
at the office of the HPEC at Shiksha Sadan, 17, Rouse Avenue, New  
Delhi, between 1400 hrs and 1600 hrs on working days.
4. The Report further states that, since there was “hardly any  
response”, a press release was issued on 17.10.2026 through print  
and electronic media, and a scroll was added to the portal from  
21.10.2026 reiterating the alternative modes of submission. 

--- Page 12 ---
11
5. However, despite these efforts, only a few complaints have  
been received on the portal, and none by e-mail, post or physical  
delivery. It is also evident from the Report that the HPEC has not  
yet returned any findings on the issues referred to it.
6. In light of the report,  we  have  impressed  upon learned  
Counsel/Senior Counsel for the parties that there is no requirement  
of disclosing the name or identity of any complainant or victim.  
Let the parties forward the entire material in their possession to  
the HPEC, whether through the portal, by e-mail, or physically, as  
they may deem appropriate, so that the HPEC may consider the same.  
Such  material  may  include  copies  of  the  writ  petitions  and  
affidavits filed before this Court, and the video clips or other  
material appended thereto. The material shall be received and dealt  
with by the HPEC subject to the confidentiality safeguards directed  
by this Court in its order dated 10.09.2026.
7. We clarify that the material and information so forwarded by  
the parties on behalf of the victims shall also be examined and  
considered by the HPEC.
8. The Member Secretary of the HPEC has, by a separate Memo dated  
31.10.2026, placed on record the resignation of Dr. L.R. Bishnoi,  
Former Director General of Police, Meghalaya, who was appointed as  
a Member of the HPEC  vide our order dated 18.08.2026. For the

THE STATE OF RAJASTHAN & ORS. RESPONDENT(S)
O R D E R
1. Leave granted.
2. The instant  appeal has been filed by the  Appellant–Wife 
assailing  the  judgment  and  final  order  dated  06.02.2025 
passed by the Rajasthan High Court at Jaipur, in D.B. Habeas  
Corpus  Petition  No.  7/2025,  whereby  the  habeas  corpus  
petition filed by her for securing the custody of her two  
minor children was dismissed.
3. By way of brief background, the Appellant–Wife and Respondent 
No. 6–Husband, Deepak Bansal, were married on 17.07.2013. Two  
children were born from the wedlock. Due to differences  
between the parties, the Court dissolved their marriage vide 
a decree of divorce by mutual consent on 30.10.2018. The  
parties  state  that  they  subsequently  remarried  in  2021; 
however,  shortly  thereafter,  disputes  resurfaced  between  
them,  leading  to  several  proceedings,  including  the  

--- Page 15 ---
2
underlying habeas corpus petition.
4. On 19.05.2025, when this matter was taken up for hearing, on  
the suggestion of learned counsel for the parties, the matter  
was referred to the Supreme Court Mediation Centre to explore  
the possibility of an amicable settlement. 
5. Pursuant thereto, the  Appellant–Wife and Respondent No. 6–
Husband have amicably resolved their disputes and have filed  
a joint application under Article 142 of the Constitution of  
India read with Section 13-B of the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955.  
Along with the application, the parties have placed on record  
the Settlement Agreement dated 28.11.2025 (Agreement), which  
is duly signed by the parties and their respective Advocates,  
besides the Mediator.
6. As per the Agreement, Respondent No. 6–Husband has agreed to  
pay a total sum of Rs. 20,00,000/- (Rupees Twenty Lakhs only)  
to the  Appellant–Wife towards full and final settlement of  
her  claims  arising  out  of  the  matrimonial  relationship. 
Pursuant thereto, a sum of Rs. 10,00,000/- (Rupees Ten Lakhs  
only) was paid by demand draft and handed over to the  
Appellant–Wife on the date of filing of the joint application  
i.e.,  16.01.2026.  The  remaining  sum  of  Rs.  10,00,000/-  
(Rupees Ten Lakhs only) has also been paid to the Appellant–
Wife by way of a demand draft dated 28.10.2026, at the time  
of hearing before this Court.
7. In terms of the Agreement, the parties have further resolved
