          UPON hearing the counsel the Court made the following
                             O R D E R
1. Application  for  permission  to  file  contempt  petition  is
allowed.
2. In view of the urgent applications alleging violation of our
earlier order dated 09.10.2026 and also alleging illegal detention
of large number of individuals at Delhi on 10.10.2026, this Court
has assembled for an urgent hearing.
3. During hearing, Mr. K.K. Venugopal, Mr. Kapil Sibal, Mr. N.
Hariharan, Mr. Gopal Sankaranarayanan, Dr. Menaka Guruswamy, Mr.
Shadan Farasat, Dr. Monika Gusain, Mr. Shoeb Alam, Mr. Sanjay
Hegde, learned senior counsel and Mr. Devvrat, learned AOR along
with other learned senior counsel/counsel submitted that there were
flagrant  violations  of  directions  given  in  our  order  dated
09.10.2026; metro railway was not functioning; New Delhi railway
station and Nizamuddin railway station remained closed; no traffic
advisories were issued and large number of persons were subjected
to undue harassment and illegal detention even beyond the area
which was covered by the prohibitory order passed under Section 163
5

--- Page 7 ---
of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (“BNSS”).  It was
also contended that there was a complete shutdown of the internet
and that the city bore a curfew like look with suspension of
transport and free movement of people and goods.
4. In addition to such submissions, oral mentionings were made in
respect of Diary No. 63952/2026, Diary No. 63943/2026 and Diary No.
63944/2026 wherein similar prayers had been made.
5. Dr. Menaka Guruswamy in support of the listed matters being
Diary  No.63801/2026  (in  Diary  No.63560/2026)  and  Diary
No.63858/2026 took us through the pleadings, wherein it has been
averred that the metro railway stations namely GTB Nagar Metro
Station and Viswavidyalaya Metro Station among other metro stations
were completely shut out to public and there has been a deliberate
and wilful violation of our order as aforesaid.
6. Addressing  us  on  the  Habeas  Corpus  petition  (Diary
No.63858/2026), Dr. Guruswamy forcibly contended that the prayer
re: illegal detention is not merely restricted to the personalities
referred to therein but is a plea against widespread illegal
detention of about 7,000 individuals in the city.
7. In rebuttal, Mr. Tushar Mehta, learned Solicitor General of
India & Mr. S.V. Raju, learned Additional Solicitor General of
India contend that the petitions which were orally mentioned have
not been served on the Union and accordingly they are unable to
assist the Court on the issues pleaded therein.
6

--- Page 8 ---
8. With  regard  to  the  allegations  made  in  the  Diary  No.
63801/2026,  Mr.  Tushar  Mehta  orally  denies  and  disputes  the
allegations of violation of order dated 09.10.2026 and contends
that the Delhi Metro was functioning normally, however, 45 out of
303 stations had been closed to maintain law and order.  In
response to the Habeas Corpus petition, he denied any one had been
illegally detained and otherwise the petition is infructuous as the
detenues referred to have since been released.
9. With regard to the submission that the essential services
still remain suspended, he assured this Court that all metro
stations are open since 10 p.m. yesterday, trains are functioning
normally and internet connection has been restored.  With regard to
a serious insinuation that Section 163 of the BNSS had been clamped
in and around the Supreme Court premises, Mr. Mehta sought time to
seek instructions.
10. Other counsel have made prayers for intervention in these
proceedings and contend that the fundamental freedoms must be
exercised  in  a  reasonable  manner  and  must  be  subjected  to
reasonable restrictions.
11. Having heard the learned senior counsel as well as the learned
Solicitor General of India and learned Additional Solicitor General
of India, we note that our order dated 09.10.2026, inter alia, held
as follows:
“7.  It  appears  that  the  notifications  are
overreaching, as they impose a complete ban upon
essential public transportation. Upon our indication
7

--- Page 9 ---
that a distinction exists between total ‘prohibition’
and  the  ‘regulation’  of  Metro  and  other  train
movements, learned Solicitor General of India has
fairly agreed to impress upon the authorities to
reassess such prohibitions. Consequently, they will
permit the operation of Metro trains and other rail
services  in  such  a  regulated  manner,  that  no
inconvenience is caused to the residents and general
public of Delhi, while adhering to the principles of
proportionality.
8. Regarding that assurance, we clarify that although
there shall be no outright prohibition on the movement
of Metro trains, a prudent decision may be made to
permit the operation of Metro trains and other rail
services in a suitable and regulated manner. Such a
decision  should  be  widely  publicised  to  prevent
further inconvenience.
9. In this vein, a traffic advisory shall also be
published in respect of smooth movement of vehicles in
newspapers and on online platforms to ensure that no
hardship or inconvenience is caused to the public in
the course of traveling within the city. The order
will be issued today to ensure that the decision
receives publicity and that there is ample clarity on
the prevailing circumstances.
10. During the course of hearing, it was suggested,
and learned senior counsel and counsel for the parties
were  ad idem , that it is high time basic amenities
were provided to those engaged in peaceful and lawful
protest.  To  that  end,  suitable  alternative  sites
capable of housing such infrastructure need to be
identified and designated. Let the Competent Authority
consider  this  suggestion  and  take  an  appropriate
decision thereon.
11. Let a compliance report be filed by the Delhi
Metro  Rail  Corporation,  the  requisite  Railway
authorities and Delhi Police (Traffic) in this regard
within four days.
12. Ordered accordingly.”
8

--- Page 10 ---
12. In summary, by the said order, firstly, the authorities
concerned had been directed to revisit the earlier notification
issued by Delhi Metro regarding closure of 57 metro stations
keeping in mind the fact that the operation of metro train and
other rail services may be regulated in appropriate situations but
not prohibited outrightly. Secondly, the order had also directed
the authorities to give wide publicity to the reconsidered decision
to avoid further inconvenience to the public.  Thirdly, the order
had directed fresh traffic advisories in newspapers and online
platforms in respect of free vehicle movement. The order has also
called upon the authorities to submit a compliance report within
four days i.e., 13.10.2026.
13. Having considered the submissions of the learned counsel for
the parties and perused the averments in the Contempt Petition, we
are of the opinion that the petitioners have made out a prima facie
case.  Accordingly, we issue notice upon the alleged contemnors.
They are directed to file counter affidavit to the said petition
including a compliance report in terms of our order as aforesaid by
13.10.2026. Their personal presence is dispensed with in the
meanwhile. Similarly, notice is issued in Habeas Corpus Petition
(Diary No.63858/2026).
14. The other unlisted mentioned petitions shall be served upon
the office of the Solicitor General during the course of the day
and  shall  be  tagged  with  the  present  matters.
Intervention/impleadment application(s) shall also be tagged with
these matters.
9

--- Page 11 ---
15. Learned counsel have strongly urged for ad-interim reliefs
with regard to smooth operation of metro rail, traffic, internet
services  and  free  movement  of  people,  especially  lawyers,
journalists  and  other  professionals.  Mr.  Mehta  has,  on
instructions, submitted that internet facilities have since been
restored and all metro railway stations are fully functional. He
also  assures  the  Court  that  lawyers,  journalists  and  other
professionals  shall  not  be  impeded  in  discharge  of  their
professional duties.
16. Taking note of such submissions, we direct as follows:
(a) Metro trains and railway facilities shall function normally
until and unless they need to be regulated keeping in mind
reasonable restrictions envisaged under Article 19(2).
(b) Similarly, free movement of people in the city shall be
ensured  subject  to  law  and  order  situation  and/or  traffic
regulations. Imposition of barricades on roads shall be reviewed
forthwith to ensure free movement subject to law and order and
traffic requirements. 
(c) Internet facilities should function unhindered.
(d) There shall be no illegal detention, particularly in cases of
peaceful unarmed assemblage of people beyond the area where Section
163 BNSS is in operation.
(e) We have been informed that a notice board stating Section 163
BNSS has been invoked in and around the precincts of the Supreme
Court has been put up in the Court premises. We direct the
authorities concerned to forthwith take down the said board, if not
10

--- Page 12 ---
already done, so that there is no apprehension with regard to
ingress and egress to the Supreme Court or any other court of law. 
(f) Lawyers, journalists and other professionals shall not be
hindered in discharge of their professional duties in any manner
whatsoever.
17. List on 13.10.2026 for further consideration along with Diary
No. 63952/2026, Diary No. 63943/2026, Diary No. 63944/2026 and
application(s) for intervention/impleadment.
