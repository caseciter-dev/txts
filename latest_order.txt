IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA 
CIVIL APPELLATE JURISDICTION 
 
CIVIL APPEAL NO. 291/2011 
 
 
 
STATE OF U.P. & ORS       APPELLANT(S) 
 
VERSUS 
 
GRAND SLAM FITNESS(P) LTD.            RESPONDENT(S) 
 
WITH 
 
CIVIL APPEAL NO. 293/2011 
 
 
ORDER 
 
 
1. Heard Mr. Bhakti Vardhan Singh and                       
Ms. Charanya Lakshmikumaran, learned counsel for the 
parties. 
2. The respondents in Civil Misc. Writ Petition 
No. 534/2009 and Civil Misc. Writ Petition No. 
361/2009 are the appellants. 
3. The Writ Petitioners are private limited 
companies incorporated under the provisions of the 
Companies Act, 1956. They are primarily engaged in 
the sale of physical exercise and fitness equipment, 
such as the Cross Elliptical Trainer, Manual 
Treadmill, Motorized Treadmill, Rowers Exercise 
Bikes, Strength Equipment, etc. The petitioners are 
registered dealers under the U.P. Value Added Tax 
Act, 2008. The companies have filed a Writ of 

--- Page 3 ---
2 
Certiorari against the order dated 18.09.2008 passed 
by the second appellant and the order dated 
26.11.2008 passed by the third appellant in the Civil 
Appeals. The subject-matter relates to the period 
from April, 2008 to July, 2008. 
4. Through the order dated 31.10.2008, the 
Revenue called upon the dealers to pay tax @ 12.5% 
under the Act on the sports equipment dealt with by 
the dealers. Here, we use the expression equipment 
inasmuch as the controversy between the Revenue and 
the dealers centres around the nature, name, and code 
entry to which it belongs, and whether the equipment 
dealt with by the dealers falls within the exemption 
granted vide notification dated 09.04.2008. 
5. To appreciate the controversy, the following 
few lines are adverted to: 
5.1 The dealers claim that the equipment falls 
under Commodity Code Entry No. 60 under Section 70 
of the Act under which the goods have been 
classified. The exemption notification dated 
09.04.2008 reads as under: 
“51. Wooden Toys, Lac + Shellac, 
including Paseva Mulamma, Button, Leo and 
Kiri, Sports Goods excluding Apparels, 
and sports Footwear, stop clock. 
A true typed copy of the Notification No. 
1021 dated 9/4 2008 is annexed herewith 
and marked as ANNEXURE P-2 to this SLP.” 

--- Page 4 ---
3 
 
6. Therefore, since the equipment dealt with by 
the dealer squarely falls within the definition of 
‘Sports Goods’, first, the levy and imposition of 
tax on exempted goods is impermissible, and second, 
levying tax @ 12.5%, which treats it as unclassified 
goods, is illegal. Hence, prayed for setting aside 
the demand. 
7. The case of Revenue is that the notification 
dated 09.04.2008 employs broad expressions, and from 
the perspective of the revenue, the subsequent 
notification issued would make it clear that, through 
the notification dated 09.04.2008, the revenue did 
not treat the equipment dealt with by the dealer as 
sports goods. Therefore, for the subsequent periods, 
the levy is tenable. 
8. The above controversy has been considered by 
the High Court in the impugned judgment. The High 
Court first referred to Section 70 of the Act and 
the commodity code assigned by the revenue. It is 
not in dispute that Entry No. 60 under the commodity 
codes deals with “sports Goods, Games and Toys”. 
Therefore, the High Court is of the view that there 
is no good or valid reason to exclude the equipment 
dealt with by the dealer from the meaning of sports 
goods. Consequently, the High Court has set aside 

--- Page 5 ---
4 
the impugned notice. 
9. The learned counsel appearing for the State 
argues that the High Court ought to have given 
meaning to the expression ‘sports goods’ in relation 
to the notification dated 09.04.2008. Under such an 
understanding, it cannot be said that the equipment 
dealt with by the dealer can be treated as sports 
goods. Therefore, it is argued that the assessments 
dated 18.09.2008 and 26.11.2008 were validly issued 
and that the High Court ought not to have interfered 
with them. 
10. Advocate Charanya Lakshmikumaran, appearing 
for the dealer, contends that the commodity entry is 
general but includes the equipment dealt with by the 
dealer. The reason assigned by the revenue for 
imposing tax @ 12.5% is completely illegal and 
unsustainable. Once the power of the revenue under 
Section 70 of the Act is exercised and the commodity 
code is prepared, denying the benefit of the 
exemption issued through notification dated 
09.04.2008 is illegal. The reasoning of the High 
Court is brief but tenable, and she prays for 
dismissal of the Civil Appeal. 
11. We have taken note of the submissions and 
perused the entries. 
13. Let us first begin with Entry No. 60 (supra), 

--- Page 6 ---
5 
which includes sports goods, games and toys. Let us 
juxtapose Entry No. 51 in Schedule-I, amended through 
the notification dated 09.04.2008. The conclusion is 
that the exemption clearly deals with sports goods. 
We have to juxtapose the exemption in the 
notification dated 09.04.2008 with Commodity Code 
No. 60, which deals with sports goods, games and 
toys. A simple juxtaposition of both the entries 
would make it clear that the equipment being dealt 
with by the dealers can be classified as sports 
goods. The impugned order is correct and has applied 
the correct provision of law and the exemption entry. 
14. We see no reason to interfere with the 
impugned order(s). 
15. The Civil Appeals are, accordingly, 
dismissed. 
16. Pending application(s), if any, shall stand 
disposed of.

1. Leave Granted.
2. Arising out of the order dated 02.05.20251 passed by
High Court of Judicature at Allahabad, allowing the writ
petition and setting aside the order dated 20.05.20242
passed by Election Tribunal3 (in short ‘Tribunal’), the
present appeal has been filed.
1  Passed in Writ C – No. – 23315 of 2024.
2  Passed in Election Petition No. – 01/2023.
3  Additional District Judge/Special Judge, SC/ST Act, Baghpat.

--- Page 12 ---
2
3. Before  the  Tribunal,  the  appellant  challenged
election  results  for  the  post  of  President,  Nagar
Panchayat, Rataul, District Baghpat. The respondent filed
the written statement. Thereafter, appellant filed an
application seeking amendments in the election petition
inter-alia alleging concealment by respondent in the
nomination form. The application was allowed by the
Tribunal vide order dated 20.05.2024, which was put to
challenge by the respondent before High Court, and was
allowed vide impugned order primarily on the ground that
the period of limitation to file the election petition
was thirty days from the date of declaration of result.
The amendment sought by the appellant inserting new
pleadings in the plaint is beyond limitation and sets-up
a new case, which cannot be permitted by allowing such
amendment. Therefore, the present appeal.
4. After having heard learned counsel for the parties
and on perusal of the material on record, it is to be

--- Page 13 ---
3
noted that the objections raised by the respondent are
legal in nature, however, in case the application for
amendment, is allowed, permitting the respondent to file
consequential amendment in written statement, if not
already filed, it would subserve the purpose without
causing any prejudice to either party, leaving the issues
open for decision at the time of final disposal.
5. In  view  of  the  above,  the  application  seeking
amendment is allowed with liberty to the respondent to
make consequential amendment in the written statement
raising all objections if not already raised. In the
facts, the Tribunal is directed to decide the petition in
accordance with law uninfluenced by the observations, if
any, of the High Court or of this Court, as expeditiously
as possible.
6. Accordingly, with the above direction, this appeal

--- Page 14 ---
4
stands disposed-of. Pending applications, if any, shall
also stand disposed of.
